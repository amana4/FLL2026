BIOGLOW · Robot design

Six teams built six robots.
The good ones all did
the same four things.

Before we build ours, let's look at what other people already worked out — and at the mistakes they only found after building. Every one of these designs was somebody's best idea at the time.

The robot that wins is not the cleverest one. It is the one that does the same thing every single time.

Spot the pattern

Three robots slip. Three don't. Why?

Look back at the table and sort the six by which wheels they use. The slipping stops exactly where the wheels change.

Design rule 1

A box frame lines you up for free

Push a box flat against the wall and it can only sit one way — square. That is a free, perfect starting position, every single run, with no code at all.

Design rule 2

Keep the weight low

Weight up high makes the robot rock when it starts, stops and turns. Rocking lifts weight off the wheels, and a wheel with no weight on it spins instead of driving.

Design rule 3

Hold the wheels straight

An axle held at one end bends under load, so the wheel leans and the robot curves. Hold it at both ends and it drives where you pointed it.

Design rule 4

Attachments should drop on, not screw on

In a match you get two and a half minutes. Every second spent wrestling an attachment is a second not scoring. Let gravity do the holding, and put a mount on the top and the front.

Our turn

What we are deciding this week

We do not have to invent a robot. We have to make these choices on purpose, and be able to say why — because that is the question the judges ask.

  • Box frame or open sides? A frame costs LEGO and buys a square start against the wall.
  • Which wheels? The only part touching the mat. Every design that fixed slipping changed the wheel.
  • Where does the hub sit? Low and sunk into the frame keeps weight on the wheels.
  • Are the axles held at both ends? If not, the robot curves and no amount of code fixes it.
  • How fast can we swap an attachment? Time it. Two and a half minutes is the whole match.
  • Top mount, front mount, or both? Two mounts means one build can do two jobs.

Write down what we choose and why, and photograph version one before we take it apart — that record is the thing the judges score hardest.
