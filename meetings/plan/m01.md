# Meeting 1 · Sun 9 Aug — Kickoff

**Block 1: Setup** · Long session (2 hrs)

> **Block goal:** Field built, base driving, expert emails out

> **The goal of Meeting 1 is not to build a robot.** It's to get the team
> excited, unblock what has a long lead time, and agree how you'll work.
>
> ✅ **Kits arrived 8 Aug**, so the usual first-meeting blocker is gone. That
> leaves **one** thing with a multi-week fuse: the expert emails. Everything
> else can be caught up; those can't.

## Agenda

### 0:00–0:15 · Watch the missions video

Start here. Not with introductions, not with rules — with the thing that makes
them want to be in the room.

Play the **Robot Game Missions Video** all the way through. Don't pause to
explain. Let them react.

Then one question: **"Which mission do you most want to solve?"** Go round the
room. Write the answers on a whiteboard; you'll come back to them at Meeting 9.

- [ ] Watch the missions video

### 0:15–0:35 · Who we are, and what BIOGLOW is

- Names, and something each person is good at (not necessarily robotics —
  drawing, organising, arguing, talking to strangers, all of it is useful)
- Fill in [`team-roster.md`](https://github.com/amana4/FLL2026/blob/main/docs/team-roster.md)
- **Pick a team name.** Fun, and it's the first real decision made together.
- Assign the **Kid 1–5 slots** in [`meeting-plan.md`](../../docs/meeting-plan.md)
  so the homework tables make sense

**Introduce the theme** — script and talking points in
[`what-is-bioglow.md`](../../docs/what-is-bioglow.md). The short version:

> **BIO** = life, **GLOW** = light and energy. A rainforest literally glows —
> bioluminescent fungi, fireflies. And a healthy ecosystem "glows" in the sense
> of thriving. This season is about keeping the glow on.

Ask them what they think GLOW means before telling them. FIRST never defines
it, so there's no wrong answer — good first team discussion.

Explain the four parts, and that **they're equally weighted**:

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Robot Game | Points on the table in 2.5 minutes |
| Robot Design | Judges ask how we built and coded it |
| Innovation Project | Find a biodiversity problem, design a solution |
| Core Values | How we work together — judged everywhere |

Worth saying out loud today: **the robot is one quarter.** Teams that discover
this in October have a bad October.

- [ ] Team name, roster, Kid 1–5 slots assigned
- [ ] Theme introduced — everyone can say what BIOGLOW is about

### 0:35–1:00 · The missions

Hand out the printed mission pages — two or three per person.

Each person reads theirs and reports back in 30 seconds:

- What does the robot have to do?
- How many points?
- What's the catch?

A reading exercise disguised as a game. By the end the team has collectively
read all 15 missions.

**Point out the three that shape strategy:**

- **M10 Fragile Microhabitats** — 20 points for *not* touching anything
- **M04 Lucky Leaves** — scores **zero** if the katydid ends up out of place
- **M07 Humongous Fungus** — both teams score if both fully extend

Full details: [`robot-game/missions/`](../../robot-game/missions/README.md)

- [ ] Split and read all 15 missions, report back

### 1:00–1:25 · Biodiversity brainstorm

Switch to the Innovation Project while energy is still high.

The brief, read aloud:

> Identify and research a problem that **puts biodiversity at risk**, and design
> an innovative solution that can help.

Go wide. Target **20+ ideas in 20 minutes**, no criticism, everything written
down. Prompts by category are in
[`brainstorm.md`](../../innovation-project/brainstorm.md) if the room goes quiet,
and FIRST's four official **Project Sparks** are in
[`what-is-bioglow.md`](../../docs/what-is-bioglow.md).

Ask: *"What's something living near us that's in trouble?"* Local beats global —
you can visit local, photograph it, and interview someone about it. The
rainforest is the *setting*; your project doesn't have to be.

- [ ] Biodiversity brainstorm — 20+ ideas

### 1:25–1:45 · Expert list ⚠️ critical path

**The single most time-sensitive task of the season.**

Explain why: experts take 2–3 weeks to reply, if they reply at all. Emails sent
today become interviews in September. Emails sent in October become nothing.

As a group, list **10 people or organisations**:

- University biology or ecology departments (PhD students reply fastest)
- Council ecology officer, parks department
- Wildlife trusts, conservation groups
- Nature reserve wardens, botanic gardens
- Zoo or aquarium education officers
- Beekeepers, farmers, foresters

Write the list into
[`expert-outreach.md`](../../innovation-project/expert-outreach.md).

**Assign two students to send the emails this week** using the template there.
Students write them, not coaches — that's part of what's judged.

- [ ] **List 10 experts**; assign 2 students to email them

### 1:45–2:00 · How we'll work

- Fill in [`team-agreement.md`](../../core-values/team-agreement.md) together
- The important question: **"How will we decide when we disagree?"** Settle it
  now, while nobody is annoyed. Much harder mid-argument in October.
- Confirm the schedule: **Sundays and Wednesdays**, 26 sessions to the
  competition

**While that discussion happens**, two students unbox and inventory the kits
against the parts list and put the hubs on charge. A good job for anyone who's
had enough talking.

- [ ] Team agreement, including how we resolve disagreements
- [ ] **Unbox the kits, inventory against the parts list, charge the hubs**
- [ ] ⚠️ **Coach: register the team**

| Kid 4 | Finish the kit inventory; list anything missing (LEGO replaces free). | ☐ |
| Kid 5 | Write up today's meeting log in [`meetings/`](../README.md). | ☐ |

## Meeting 1 is a success if…

- [ ] Everyone wants to come back
- [ ] Team is registered
- [ ] 10 expert targets identified, 2 people assigned to email them
- [ ] Kits inventoried, hubs charging
- [ ] The team has a name
- [ ] Everyone knows the robot is only 25%

Only one of those is about the robot. That's deliberate — with kits already
here it's tempting to spend the whole session building, and the teams that do
are the ones scrambling on the project in October.
