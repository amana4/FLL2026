# After the Qualifier

The plan ends at Meeting 26 on 4 November. If the team advances, there is another
event weeks later and no plan for it. Worth thinking about in September rather than
finding out in November.

## How advancement works

From the official Awards and Allocation document:

> "If applicable, Champion's Rank is also used to identify teams that will advance to
> the next level of competition."

So advancement follows the **Champion's Rank**, which is the sum of your four area
ranks with the lowest total first. Not the robot score. A team that advances is
usually one that was solid across all four areas rather than one that won the Robot
Performance award.

"If applicable" is doing real work in that sentence — whether advancement exists at
all, and how many teams advance, depends entirely on the region.

- [ ] Ask the regional partner: does our qualifier advance teams, and how many?
- [ ] If so, what is the championship date and where?
- [ ] Is there an additional entry fee?

Also worth remembering from the participation rules: **"Teams are only eligible for
awards and advancement at their initial qualifying event within each tournament
level."** You get one shot per level.

## What families need to know now, not in November

This is the practical reason to sort it early. If the team advances:

- There is **another full Saturday**, typically two to six weeks later
- It may involve **travel and possibly an overnight stay**
- There is likely an **extra fee**
- The team keeps meeting through that gap

None of that is in the current plan or the current budget. The
[team fund](https://github.com/amana4/FLL2026/blob/main/docs/team-fund.md) lists
travel for "early November" only.

So tell parents in September that advancement is possible, roughly what it would cost,
and roughly when. A family that has booked something for that weekend would rather
know in September.

## What changes between events, and what does not

**The design freeze holds.** Meeting 19 froze the robot on 11 October for good
reasons, and those reasons do not stop applying because you advanced. Resist
rebuilding. Teams that redesign between a qualifier and a championship usually arrive
slower and less reliable.

**What is worth changing:**

- Fix whatever actually broke on the day, and nothing else
- Act on the judges' written feedback — you get the rubrics and the feedback sheet
  back at the end of the event, and it says exactly where you lost marks
- Rehearse the judging session more. It is the cheapest available improvement.
- Get the Innovation Project in front of one more person, since ITERATE rewards
  sharing with multiple groups
- Practise more full matches, because reliability is where scores actually come from

**What is not worth changing:** the mission list, the attachments that work, the
presentation structure.

## If you advance, plan the gap

Between the two events, keep the twice-weekly rhythm but change what it is for:

| Session | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Read the returned rubrics and feedback together. List every gap. |
| 2 | Fix only what broke on the day |
| 3 | Full matches, timed, scored |
| 4 | Judging rehearsal with a new mock judge |
| 5 | Share the project with one more group |
| 6 | Full matches again |
| Last | Dress rehearsal, then pack |

Copy the shape of Meetings 23 to 26 rather than inventing something new.

## If you do not advance

Which is the likely outcome for a first-year team of five, and is completely fine.

Do the [retrospective](../docs/retrospective.md) while it is fresh, in the week after. It is
the most valuable hour of the whole season for whoever coaches next year — and it is
much better written in November than reconstructed in July.

Then look at the three awards aimed at teams like ours: Breakthrough, Rising All-Star
and Motivate. Not winning Champion's in year one is the normal experience of almost
every team that later wins it.

## The feedback sheet is the real prize

Whatever happens, you leave with the two rubrics and the written feedback page:

> "The rubrics and feedback page will be returned to teams at the end of the event."

That is a professional assessment of your team's work against published criteria,
written by people who saw thirty other teams the same day. Read it together, put it in
the notebook, and use it as the starting point for next season.
