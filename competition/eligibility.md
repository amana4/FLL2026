# Eligibility and Participation Rules

Quoted from the official Participation Rules, updated September 2024. These are the
rules that decide whether the team can compete at all, so they are worth checking
before anything else.

## Check this now

> "Participants must not be younger than **9 years old** or older than **14 years
> old** for US/Canada."

Outside the US and Canada the range is 9 to 16. Regions may also use grade rather
than age:

> "Team members are between the minimum and maximum age or grade allowed in their
> region."

**Our team is described as roughly 9 to 14, which straddles the upper boundary.**
Eligibility is verified at registration, so if anyone is 14 now and turns 15 before
the event, or is already 15, that needs resolving with the regional partner today
rather than in October.

- [ ] Every student's age confirmed against the regional rule
- [ ] Regional partner asked whether they use age or grade

## Team size

> "A competitive team consists of at least two (2) and a maximum of ten (10)
> children."

Five is comfortably inside that. It also means the team could take on a sixth
student without breaking any rule — though it would break the five-slot rotation
table, so see [onboarding a new member](../docs/new-member.md).

Adult supervision is required as described in the FIRST Youth Protection Policy.

## Everyone attends everything

> "**All team members attending an event must participate in the judging session and
> be present at the Robot Game matches.**"

That is bold in the original. It interacts with something important: there is
[one judging session](../docs/judging/README.md), about 30 minutes, and all five
students are in that room together. Nobody sits it out, which is why the judging
prep insists that everyone can speak to any part of the work.

## Registration

> "A team must be registered and fully paid in their national registration system to
> sign up for official events. Additional regional/local event fees may apply."

Season materials are only available to teams with a paid registration, and they are
released on the first Tuesday in August.

- [ ] Registered and fully paid nationally
- [ ] Regional or local event fee checked and paid — this is separate

## You only get one qualifying event

> "Teams are only eligible for awards and advancement at their **initial qualifying
> event** within each tournament level."

So you cannot enter a second qualifier hoping for a better result. Whichever event
you attend first is the one that counts, which makes the choice of event worth a
moment's thought if there is more than one option.

## Allowed hardware is broader than you might assume

> "One of the following LEGO robot sets: a LEGO Education SPIKE Prime, LEGO
> MINDSTORMS Robot Set, LEGO MINDSTORMS Robot Inventor and equivalent NXT, RCX or
> EV3."

We are on SPIKE Prime. Worth knowing that an older EV3 in a cupboard is legal, if a
spare is ever needed.

The Robot Game Rulebook narrows this further for a match: one controller, four
motors, and only touch/force, colour, distance and gyro sensors. See
[equipment limits](../robot-game/README.md).

## What can get you thrown out

The consequences section is short and specific:

> "Individuals interfering with, excessively instructing, prompting, or heckling a
> team or volunteer **may be asked to leave**."

> "Severe infractions of these rules may result in a **team's dismissal** from the
> event."

> "Individuals and/or teams who fail to abide by the Participation Rules may be
> **ineligible for awards**."

Note that the first one is about adults, and that "excessively instructing" a team
counts. Volunteers are trained to spot it. This is covered properly in
[how much help is allowed](../docs/coaching.md), and it is the single most important
thing for a well-meaning parent-engineer to read.

## The rule underneath all the others

> "**All work presented at events is the work of the children on the team.**"

Everything else in the participation rules follows from that one sentence.

## Class Packs, for reference

Some schools run FLL as a Class Pack rather than a competitive team. Not our route,
but two facts worth knowing:

> "One team emerging from a Class Pack is eligible to participate in qualifying
> tournaments."

> "Students who are part of a Class Pack may also join a competitive team."
