# Awards

Eleven awards exist. Nothing in this repo mentioned any of them until now, which is
a shame — three are aimed squarely at a first-year team, and one is nominated by the
students themselves.

All wording below is quoted from the official Awards and Allocation document.

## How the Champion's Award is actually decided

This is worth understanding before anything else, because it changes strategy.

The four areas are weighted equally, but the Champion's Award does not add up your
*scores*. It adds up your **ranks**:

```
INNOVATION PROJECT RANK
      + CORE VALUES RANK
      + ROBOT DESIGN RANK
      + ROBOT GAME RANK
      = CHAMPION'S SCORE          ← lowest total wins
```

> "The four area ranks are weighted equally and used to calculate the Champion's
> Score … The team with the lowest Champion's Score will be ranked first."

**Why this matters.** Being solid in all four beats being outstanding in one and
weak in three. A team ranked 5th, 6th, 5th and 6th scores 22. A team ranked 1st,
1st, 20th and 20th scores 42 and loses badly. There is no way to buy your way out of
a weak area with a strong one, which is the real argument for protecting the
Innovation Project time.

Champion's Rank is also what decides **advancement**, where advancement applies.

### How each area score is built

| Area | Built from |
| --- | --- |
| Innovation Project | All ten scores from the Innovation Project rubric |
| Robot Design | All ten scores from the Robot Design rubric |
| Core Values | Five designated criteria from **each** rubric (ten scores), plus three Gracious Professionalism scores |
| Robot Game | Your best of at least three matches |

Gracious Professionalism scores feed Core Values but "do not affect a team's Robot
Game Score or rank".

All judging evaluations and rankings stay confidential.

## Required awards

Each has 1 winner and up to 3 finalists, depending on tournament size.

**Champion's Award** — "celebrates a team that embodies the FIRST LEGO League
Challenge experience by fully embracing the Core Values while achieving excellence
and innovation in Robot Performance, Robot Design, and the Innovation Project."

**Core Values Award** — "displays extraordinary enthusiasm and spirit; exemplifies
teamwork, discovery, inclusion, innovation, impact, and fun; and shows each other
and other teams respect at all times."

**Innovation Project Award** — "utilizes diverse resources … has a creative,
well-researched solution; and effectively communicates their findings to judges and
the community."

**Robot Design Award** — "uses outstanding programming principles and solid
engineering practices to develop a robot that is mechanically sound, durable,
efficient, and highly capable."

**Robot Performance Award** — 1 winner and up to 3 finalists (2nd, 3rd and 4th).
"Celebrates a team that scores the most points during the Robot Game. Teams have a
chance to compete in at least three 2.5-minute matches, and their highest score
counts."

**Coach/Mentor Award** — up to 6 winners. "Goes to the coach or mentor whose
leadership and guidance is clearly evident and best exemplifies the FIRST Core
Values."

## Optional awards, up to 3 winners each

Which of these an event runs is up to the organiser, so **ask which are being
used**. Three of the five are realistic for a rookie team.

**Breakthrough Award** — "a team that made significant progress in their confidence
and capability in at least one of the core areas." Realistic for us.

**Rising All-Star Award** — "a team that the judges notice and expect great things
from in the future." Realistic for us.

**Motivate Award** — "embraces the culture of FIRST LEGO League through team
building, team spirit, and displayed enthusiasm." Realistic for us.

**Engineering Excellence Award** — "an efficiently designed robot, an innovative
project solution … and Core Values evident in all they do."

**Peer Award** — "nominated by their peers for their expression of Core Values and
Gracious Professionalism." Won by how you treat other teams, which is worth saying
to five children on the day.

## The one the students can win for you

`coach-mentor-nomination-print.pdf` is a printable form the **team** fills in to
nominate their own coach for the Coach/Mentor Award. It asks who is nominating
(team member, family member, fellow coach, event volunteer), and then:

> "This coach/mentor inspires my team or others by… Write or draw your response
> here."

Instruction on the form: **"Return this completed form to an event volunteer."**

It needs printing before the event and it is now on the
[packing list](packing-list.md). Coaches should probably not be the ones to
remember this, which is exactly why it is written down here.

## What to tell the team

Say early that Champion's is decided by summing ranks, so consistency across all
four areas is the goal, not brilliance in one.

And say that Breakthrough, Rising All-Star and Motivate exist. A first-year team of
five with a well-documented notebook and genuine enthusiasm is a real candidate for
those, and knowing there is something to aim for other than first place changes how
October feels.

## Ask the organiser

- [ ] Which optional awards is this event running?
- [ ] How many finalists per award, given the tournament size?
- [ ] Does advancement apply, and how many teams advance?
