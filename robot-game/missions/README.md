# Missions

All 15 BIOGLOW missions, plus the Equipment Inspection bonus and Precision
Tokens. Point values come from the official Robot Game Rulebook (pp. 8–12); the
piece counts come from the referee's scoresheet, which is what fixes the maximums
on the per-item missions.

> **Check [Challenge Updates](../../docs/official-materials/README.md) monthly.**
> They supersede the rulebook. None as of 4 August 2026.

## The two scores that are not missions

| Source | Max | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equipment Inspection | 20 | Everything fits in **one** launch area, under the height limit |
| Precision Tokens | 50 | Six tokens; you keep points for what is left |
| | **Total available: 530** | |

**530 is the theoretical maximum.** Nobody scores it. It is useful only as the
denominator when the team sets a target at Meeting 9.

### Equipment Inspection — 20 points

> "If your robot and all equipment fit completely in one launch area and under a
> height limit of 12 in. (305 mm) during this inspection: 20 points"

Note carefully what is a rule and what is a bonus, because they differ. **All
equipment must fit within the two launch areas and under 12 in.** — that is a hard
requirement the referee checks. Fitting it all into **one** launch area is what
earns the 20 points.

This interacts with **M13**: the keystone species you build yourself counts as
equipment and is inspected too, so build it compact.

### Precision Tokens — up to 50 points

Six tokens worth 50. Interrupt the robot outside home and the referee removes one.

| Tokens left | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Points** | 50 | 50 | 35 | 25 | 15 | 10 | 0 |

**The first interruption is free** — six and five both score 50. After that it
gets expensive fast: the second costs 15, the third 10, the fourth 10. A run
needing two rescues costs 25 points before it scores anything, which is why
reliability is a scoring strategy and not just a convenience.

Tokens are managed by the referee.

## The No Equipment Constraint

Eight missions carry a crossed-out-brick symbol in the top-right corner of their
rulebook card. Where it appears:

> "A mission model cannot earn points if it is touching equipment at the end of
> the match. This rule applies only to the specific mission in which the
> constraint appears. If the same mission model is used in another mission where
> the constraint does not apply, it can earn points as usual."

**Applies to: M01, M04, M05, M07, M09, M10, M12, M15.**
**Does not apply to: M02, M03, M06, M08, M11, M13, M14.**

This is a "left the attachment behind and lost the mission" trap. If the robot
drops or parks anything touching one of those eight models, the points go — even
though the mission looks completed. Check it at the end of every practice run, not
just on the day.

Note the asymmetry: M06's nest can be touching equipment and still score, but
M10's habitats cannot.

| One action, one condition | M08, M11, M13 | 80 |

## Things worth knowing before you choose

**M10 is 20 points for doing nothing.** Both habitats start in position; you keep
the points by not disturbing them. It appears in two places on the field, so check
it against every route. It also carries the NEC, so nothing may be touching it at
the end.

**M08 is 30 points for one condition** — the best single-action value on the field.

**M06 and M14 are worth 40 each**, more than they look, because they are per-item
across four pieces.

**M04 can score zero.** If the katydid ends up outside the leaf habitat, even
partly, the whole mission scores nothing. And the referee randomises the katydid
and leaf positions at the start of every match, so a fixed program cannot solve it.

**M07 needs your opponent.** Up to two connections, 10 each, so up to 20 added.
Both teams earn it if both fully extend. Mention it to your match partner before
the round — free points for both of you.

**M13 to M15 sit on the three interchangeable docks** (mine, city, farm) and you
choose the placement before the match. M15's bonus depends on that choice, and the
referee records the dock on the scoresheet. Put M15 on the dock matching whichever
of its three actions your robot does most reliably.

## Filling in the analysis

Each mission page has an analysis table to complete during Meetings 3 to 6:

1. Build the model — bag numbers are in the table above
2. Time how long your robot needs
3. Compute points-per-second, which is what should drive the run plan
4. Roll up into [`../scoring-tracker.md`](../scoring-tracker.md)
5. Group into runs in [`../strategy.md`](../strategy.md), by field location
