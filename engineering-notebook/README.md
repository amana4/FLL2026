# Engineering Notebook

FIRST supplies the notebook as a **32-page workbook the team writes in**, and it is
in the repo as `docs/official-materials/fll-challenge-bioglow-en.pdf`. It walks
through FIRST's 12 sessions with prompts and worksheets.

It is **not required and not submitted.** Official wording, page 3:

> "This notebook is a great resource to share at your judging event, but it isn't
> required."

So there is no deadline, no page limit, and no portal to upload to. What the
notebook is for is bringing into the judging room and showing judges — during the
2-minute setup you lay it out with the prototype and the robot, and it becomes the
evidence behind your answers.

Note: a few regions add their own notebook requirement. **Confirm with your
regional partner**, and if they do want one, put the real deadline here.

## Why bother, then

Because the two rubrics both score **Iterate**, and Iterate means showing what
changed and why. A notebook full of dated sketches, failed versions and test
numbers answers "what broke and how did you fix it?" better than memory does. A
team that can hand over a page showing attachment v1 next to v3 is making the
judge's job easy.

The notebook is also where the official worksheets live — the Pseudocode
Worksheet with its robot path diagram, the Innovation Project Planning page, the
Team Progress checkpoints. Judges recognise those because FIRST wrote them.

## Guidance

- **Students write it.** Judges can tell when an adult did, and "all work
  presented at events is the work of the children on the team" is an official
  participation rule. Rough student writing scores better than polished adult
  writing.
- **Show the mess.** Sketches, crossed-out ideas, photos of broken attachments.
- **Date everything.** The progression is the point.
- **Photos over paragraphs.** A photo of v1 beside v3 beats a page of text.
- **Write in the FIRST workbook** rather than building something separate. The
  prompts are good and the format is familiar to judges.

## For the judging room

- [ ] Notebook up to date and brought on the day
- [ ] Laid out during the 2-minute setup, alongside the prototype and robot
- [ ] Everyone knows which page to turn to for "what failed and what changed"
