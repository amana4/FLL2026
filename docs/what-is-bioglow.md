# Introducing BIOGLOW to the Team

A script for the first 20 minutes. Written to be read aloud or paraphrased.

## What does "BIOGLOW" mean?

**BIO** = life. **GLOW** = light, energy, life showing itself.

> ⚠️ **Honest note:** FIRST never spells out what GLOW stands for. The season
> materials just use the name. So the reading below is *ours*, not official —
> which is fine, and actually a good first team discussion. Ask the kids what
> they think it means before offering an answer.

Two readings that both work:

**1. A rainforest literally glows.** Bioluminescent fungi light up the forest
floor at night. Fireflies signal to each other. Some millipedes and mushrooms
glow. Life makes its own light.

**2. A healthy ecosystem "glows" — it thrives.** FIRST's own tagline is
*"Engineer a thriving planet with BIOGLOW."* When biodiversity is healthy, the
whole system is vibrant. When it's damaged, it dims.

The season is about **keeping the glow on**.

## The setting: a rainforest and a nearby city

Straight from the rulebook:

> Get ready to explore the living systems that make our planet thrive. Your team
> will journey through a dynamic rainforest and nearby city, discovering how
> plants, animals, fungi, and people are connected in powerful ways.
>
> As you explore the missions, you'll uncover hidden relationships beneath the
> forest floor, observe species in their natural habitats, and discover how
> ecosystems grow, adapt, and recover.

## The four rainforest layers

The official materials organise the rainforest into four layers. Worth drawing
on a whiteboard — it makes the missions click:

```
   ☀️  EMERGENT LAYER    the tallest trees, poking above everything
  🌳  CANOPY LAYER      the dense green roof where most life lives
 🌿  UNDERSTORY         shady, humid, between trunks
🍄  FOREST FLOOR        dark, decomposing, fungal networks
```

Then ask: **"Which layer do you think most of our missions are in?"**

Look at the mission list together — drones survey *above* the canopy (M01),
research platforms go *into* it (M09), and mycelium and roots work *below* the
floor (M05, M07, M11). The missions climb the whole rainforest.

## The one-sentence version

> Life on Earth is connected in ways we mostly can't see — and it's in trouble.
> This season we build a robot that mimics how scientists study and protect it,
> and we find one real biodiversity problem near us and try to solve it.

## Make it concrete: what "biodiversity" actually means

Abstract word. Make it physical.

**The jenga demo (2 minutes, memorable).** Build a small tower. Each block is a
species. Pull one out — it stands. Pull another — still stands. Keep pulling.
At some point it collapses, and *you can't predict which block does it*.

That's biodiversity: an ecosystem tolerates some loss, then suddenly doesn't.

**Then ask:** *"What lives near us that most people never notice?"* Push past
the obvious answers — beyond birds and squirrels to fungi, soil life, moths,
solitary bees, moss. This question feeds straight into the Innovation Project
brainstorm.

## Connect the missions to real science

Each mission mirrors something real. Say so — it makes the robot feel less like
a game and more like the thing it's modelling:

| Mission | Real-world science |
| --- | --- |
| M01 Drone Survey | Scientists use LiDAR drones to map canopies they can't climb |
| M02 Exploding Seeds | Some plants fire seeds ballistically to spread them |
| M06 Leafcutter Frenzy | Leafcutter ants farm fungus underground — actual agriculture |
| M07 Humongous Fungus | Mycelium networks connect trees; they trade nutrients |
| M09 Research Platform | Camera traps and canopy platforms are standard field tools |
| M12 Forest Elder | Old trees really do get physical supports to keep them alive |
| M13 Keystone Species | Remove a keystone species and the ecosystem restructures |
| M15 Biocentric Architecture | Buildings designed with nesting sites and green roofs |

**M07 is worth dwelling on.** The mycelium mission only scores its bonus if
*both* teams extend their models — mirroring how fungal networks connect
separate trees. The game design makes cooperation literal.

## Official Project Sparks

If the brainstorm stalls, FIRST provides four starting prompts:

1. Scientists use **drones** to reach places humans can't — dense canopy,
   flooded ground. *How could you use aerial technology to help a rainforest?*
2. Ecologists use **simple tools** to study how plants grow and support
   wildlife. *How could you use tools innovatively to support a local ecosystem?*
3. Wildlife biologists use **camera traps and trackers** to study animals.
   *How could you use tools innovatively to support a local ecosystem?*
4. Conservationists use **mapping technology** to see how forests change.
   *How could you restore a piece of land to benefit native plants and animals?*

> **But local beats exotic.** You can't interview a rainforest. You *can*
> interview a council ecologist about the park down the road. A local problem
> gets you real research, real photos, and real feedback — all of which the
> rubric rewards. Rainforest is the setting; your project doesn't have to be.

## Questions that get kids talking

- What's the strangest living thing you've ever seen?
- If every insect vanished tomorrow, what would happen?
- What's something alive near your house that you never think about?
- Why should anyone care if one species disappears?
- What would our town look like if nature were designed in from the start?

## Official team roles

FIRST suggests these in the Team Meeting Guide. With 5 kids, double up — and
**rotate**, because the guide is explicit that the goal is a team "confident and
capable in all aspects."

| Role | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| **Team Captain** | Shares progress with the coach; makes sure tasks get finished |
| **Creative Designer** | Sketches design ideas for the team to discuss |
| **Researcher** | Investigates ideas, finds sources, informs decisions |
| **Communicator** | Focuses on how we explain our work; writes scripts and presentations |
| **Project Manager** | Time management and event preparation |
| **Material Manager** | Gathers and returns materials each session |

These map onto the Kid 1–5 slots in
[`meeting-plan.md`](meeting-plan.md) — use whichever names the team prefers.

## What not to do

**Don't lecture.** Fifteen minutes of rainforest facts loses the room. Ask
questions and let them talk.

**Don't decide the project today.** Go wide first. The decision is Meeting 7.

**Don't promise the robot will do all 15 missions.** It won't, and that's
normal — but it's demoralising to walk that back in September.
