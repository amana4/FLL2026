# Safety

Short, because most of it is obvious once written down. The reason it needs writing
down is that two things in this repo recommend building a table from timber with
children in the room, and until now said nothing about tools.

## Batteries

SPIKE Prime hubs contain a lithium battery. They are reliable, and the failure modes
are rare but worth knowing.

- **Do not charge unattended overnight.** Charge during meetings or while someone is
  in the room.
- **A swollen, dented or punctured battery is finished.** Stop using it. Do not
  charge it. Do not put it in normal household waste — batteries go to a
  battery-recycling point.
- Hubs get warm in use. Warm is normal; hot enough that you want to put it down is
  not, and usually means a motor is stalling.
- Use the LEGO charger and cable. Cheap USB chargers vary more than they should.
- If a battery ever vents or smells sharp, put it somewhere non-flammable away from
  people and tell an adult immediately.

## Motors

- A stalled motor draws current and heats up. If a mechanism jams, stop the program
  rather than letting it push.
- Do not force a motor by hand while it is powered.
- Fingers out of gear trains. Small gears in a Technic mechanism pinch.

## Building the competition table

`equipment.md` suggests building a table and border walls from timber. That is an
**adult job**, done away from the team.

- Cutting and drilling happens without children present
- Eye protection when cutting
- Sand the cut edges, because a splinter on a table edge finds a forearm eventually
- Check for protruding screws before anyone leans on it

If nobody wants to build one, practise on the floor and borrow a table for a
practice session. It is a real disadvantage — robots behave differently on tables —
but it is not worth an injury.

## Prototyping the Innovation Project

The project usually involves making something.

- Scissors and craft knives: adult supervision, and cut away from yourself
- Hot glue guns burn. They are the most common injury in a school makerspace.
- Spray paint and solvents outdoors or by an open window
- Anything involving soil, pond water or plants: wash hands afterwards
- If the project involves visiting a site — a reserve, a river, a farm — an adult
  goes, and someone knows where you are

## At the venue

- Walking pace. Gyms have cables and other people's robots.
- Do not reach across another team's table, ever. Ask.
- Know where the exits and the first-aid point are. Look on arrival.
- Keep bags under your own pit table so nobody trips.
- Stay hydrated. Gyms get hot and the day is long.

## Medication, allergies and needs

The [packing list](../competition/packing-list.md) mentions medication in one line
with no owner. That is not good enough for a full day away.

- [ ] Each family tells the coach about allergies, asthma, medication or anything else
      relevant, before the first event
- [ ] Whoever holds medication on the day is named in advance
- [ ] Emergency contacts are on the roster and the coach has them on the day
- [ ] Anyone with a food allergy knows what lunch arrangements are

Nobody needs to explain a medical need in detail. Just tell the coach what to do if
something happens.

## Youth protection

FIRST requires adult supervision under its Youth Protection Policy, and the
participation rules refer to it directly. In practice, for us:

- Never a single adult alone with a single child
- Meetings happen in an agreed, visible place
- Adults do not contact students privately; team communication goes through the
  parents' group

## If something goes wrong

1. Stop what is happening.
2. Tell an adult. At an event, tell a volunteer — they are trained and there are
   plenty of them.
3. Write it down afterwards in the meeting log, including near-misses. A near-miss
   written down is how the next one gets avoided.
