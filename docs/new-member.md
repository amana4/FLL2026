# Joining Part-Way Through

For a student joining after the season has started, and for whoever is settling them
in. Teams gain and lose members in September routinely.

## If you have just joined — read these five, in this order

1. **[What is BIOGLOW?](what-is-bioglow.md)** — the theme, the four rainforest
   layers, and what the season is about. 10 minutes.
2. **[The missions](../robot-game/missions/README.md)** — all 15, with points. Skim
   the index, then read properly the two or three the team is working on.
3. **[Meeting plan](meeting-plan.md)** — where the season is up to, and what happens
   next. Look at the key dates.
4. **[Innovation Project](../innovation-project/README.md)** — the problem the team
   chose and why. Read `problem.md` and `research.md`.
5. **[Glossary](glossary.md)** — for when someone says "square up on the line before
   the dock" and it means nothing.

Then read the last two or three **[meeting logs](../meetings/README.md)**. They are
the fastest way to understand what the team has actually been arguing about.

## What you own by the end of your second meeting

Not "watch and learn". Pick something small and real:

- One mission's analysis page, filled in
- One attachment, built and tested
- The meeting log for one session
- One expert to research and email

Owning something specific beats shadowing, and it gets you into the design log, which
is what judges read.

## What nobody will tell you unless you ask

- The robot is only **a quarter** of the score. Most of the season is the project,
  how the team works, and explaining the design.
- **All five students go into the judging room** and judges pick who to ask. So you
  will need to be able to explain parts you did not personally build. That is normal
  and everyone is in the same position.
- Ticking boxes on the website only saves to your own device. Real decisions get
  written into the markdown.
- "I don't know, but my teammate measured it" is a good answer to a judge.

## For the team settling someone in

**Give them a buddy** for the first three sessions — one named person to ask
anything, so they are not choosing which of five people to interrupt.

**Re-cut the rotation.** The rotation table in
[`meeting-plan.md`](meeting-plan.md) hard-codes five slots. Adding a sixth breaks it,
and so does losing one. It is a table, not a law — redraw it so everybody still
touches every area before the design freeze in October.

| Team size | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | Drop one focus per cycle; notebook doubles up with strategy |
| 5 | As written |
| 6 | Two students share one focus each cycle, swapping who leads |

Team size limits are 2 to 10, so any of these is legal — see
[eligibility](../competition/eligibility.md).

**Fix the money.** The [team fund](https://github.com/amana4/FLL2026/blob/main/docs/team-fund.md) assumes five families at $200.
A sixth changes the arithmetic, and someone joining in October should not be asked
for a full share.

**Update the homework tables.** All 26 meeting pages assign work to Kid 1 to Kid 5.
Adding a Kid 6 means either editing 26 files or, more sensibly, assigning the new
student alongside an existing slot until the next rotation.

**Say the theme out loud again.** Do not assume they picked it up. Ten minutes on
what BIOGLOW is, from someone who has been there since August, is worth more than any
document.

## If somebody leaves

- Re-cut the rotation the same way
- Check nothing was solely theirs — a program only they understood, an expert only
  they had contact with
- Refund their share of the fund, pro-rata, and say so publicly so nobody wonders
- Note it in the meeting log. A team that got smaller and adapted is a genuine
  Core Values story, and judges ask about setbacks.

## Checklist

- [ ] Buddy assigned for the first three sessions
- [ ] Five reading pages sent
- [ ] Something specific handed over to own
- [ ] Rotation table redrawn
- [ ] Fund arithmetic updated
- [ ] Roster updated, with pronouns asked rather than assumed
- [ ] Ages still inside the regional eligibility range
