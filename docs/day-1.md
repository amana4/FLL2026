# Day 1 — First Team Meeting

A 2-hour agenda for the first session. Print it, or open it on a laptop.

> **The goal of Day 1 is not to build a robot.** It's to get the team excited,
> unblock what has a long lead time, and agree how you'll work.
>
> ✅ **Kits arrived 8 Aug**, so the usual Day 1 blocker is gone. That leaves
> **one** thing with a multi-week fuse: the expert emails. Everything else can
> be caught up; those can't.

## 0:00–0:15 · Watch the missions video

Start here. Not with introductions, not with rules — with the thing that makes
them want to be in the room.

Play the **Robot Game Missions Video** all the way through. Don't pause to
explain. Let them react.

Then one question: **"Which mission do you most want to solve?"** Go round the
room. Write the answers on a whiteboard; you'll come back to them in Week 3.

## 0:15–0:35 · Who we are

- Names, and something each person is good at (not necessarily robotics —
  drawing, organising, arguing, talking to strangers, all of it is useful)
- Fill in [`team-roster.md`](https://github.com/amana4/FLL2026/blob/main/docs/team-roster.md)
- **Pick a team name.** Fun, and it's the first real decision made together.

Explain the four parts, and that **they're equally weighted**:

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Robot Game | Points on the table in 2.5 minutes |
| Robot Design | Judges ask how we built and coded it |
| Innovation Project | Find a biodiversity problem, design a solution |
| Core Values | How we work together — judged everywhere |

Worth saying out loud on Day 1: **the robot is one quarter.** Teams that
discover this in October have a bad October.

## 0:35–1:00 · The missions

Hand out the printed mission pages — one or two per person.

Each person reads theirs and reports back in 30 seconds:
- What does the robot have to do?
- How many points?
- What's the catch?

This is a reading exercise disguised as a game, and it works. By the end the
team has collectively read all 15 missions.

**Point out the three that shape strategy:**

- **M10 Fragile Microhabitats** — 20 points for *not* touching anything
- **M04 Lucky Leaves** — scores **zero** if the katydid ends up out of place
- **M07 Humongous Fungus** — both teams score if both fully extend

Full details: [`robot-game/missions/`](../robot-game/missions/README.md)

## 1:00–1:25 · Biodiversity brainstorm

Switch to the Innovation Project while energy is still high.

The brief, read aloud:

> Identify and research a problem that **puts biodiversity at risk**, and design
> an innovative solution that can help.

Then go wide. Target **20+ ideas in 20 minutes**, no criticism, everything
written down. Prompts by category are in
[`brainstorm.md`](../innovation-project/brainstorm.md) if the room goes quiet.

Ask: *"What's something living near us that's in trouble?"* Local beats global —
you can visit local, photograph it, and interview someone about it.

## 1:25–1:45 · Expert list ⚠️ critical path

**The single most time-sensitive task of the season.**

Explain why: experts take 2–3 weeks to reply, if they reply at all. Emails sent
today become interviews in September. Emails sent in October become nothing.

As a group, list **10 people or organisations** to contact:

- University biology or ecology departments (PhD students reply fastest)
- Council ecology officer, parks department
- Wildlife trusts, conservation groups
- Nature reserve wardens, botanic gardens
- Zoo or aquarium education officers
- Beekeepers, farmers, foresters

Write the list into
[`expert-outreach.md`](../innovation-project/expert-outreach.md).

**Assign two students to send the emails this week** using the template there.
Students write them, not coaches — that's part of what's judged.

## 1:45–2:00 · How we'll work

- Fill in [`team-agreement.md`](../core-values/team-agreement.md) together
- The important question: **"How will we decide when we disagree?"** Settle it
  now, while nobody is annoyed. It's much harder mid-argument in October.
- Meeting schedule: **Sundays and Wednesdays** until the competition —
  26 sessions, mapped out in [`meeting-plan.md`](meeting-plan.md)
- Log today in [`meetings/`](../meetings/README.md)

**While that discussion happens**, two students unbox and inventory the kits
against the parts list and put the hubs on charge. It's a good job for anyone
who's had enough talking.

do are the ones scrambling on the project in October.
